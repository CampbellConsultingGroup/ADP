This one uses a single heat scale rather than TIME's two axes — each capability is rated by how much of a gap it represents relative to how much the objective depends on it, grouped under the L1 domains they'd sit under in a capability model.

A few things this surfaces that TIME didn't make as visible:

Master data management sits alone in its own domain as critical — it's easy to fold into "inventory management" and lose it, but it's the capability most likely to be under-resourced because it wasn't named in the original problem statement.
Point of sale is amber here rather than the "migrate" verdict from the TIME map — the capability model view separates "is this capability healthy" from "is it scoped correctly," which TIME collapses into one placement. Worth keeping both views, since they answer different questions: TIME tells portfolio/investment teams what to do with a system, the capability heat map tells the business architecture what to prioritize funding for.
Customer service is green — a reminder that not everything touched by the problem needs investment; some capabilities just need the upstream fix to relieve pressure on them.

If this were live in ADP, this is the view I'd expect to render directly off the Capability model's heat attribute (which you'd set from the StrategicObjective links from the form we built earlier) rather than being hand-assigned like it is here.
